# 014 — A dedicated grounding retriever: passages and keyword scoring, not the search bar's matcher

> **Scope of the live model checks: Ollama and `gemma2:9b` only.** Retrieval itself is deterministic and model-independent (the eval needs no model). But every statement here about how a model behaves with the grounded context (irrelevant context derailing the reply, instructions to ignore it changing nothing, "I can't see your notes" in about 3 of 5 runs, the honest declines) was observed on `gemma2:9b` through Ollama, and other models, especially larger cloud ones, may behave differently. The precision-over-recall choice is tuned to that small model's weakness and is worth revisiting per model. The prompt-size cost of the larger grounding block is measured in ADR 015.

## Context

Vault grounding, the engine finding real notes to put in front of the model each turn, is the project's non-negotiable guarantee, and the model is only as honest as the evidence it is handed. ADR 002 chose to reuse the search bar's matcher for it, and ADR 006 wrapped it in a multi-query workaround. Live testing on `gemma2:9b` after the soul work showed that evidence is weak, and reading the code found five causes: snippets are search-bar sized (one line, capped at 70 characters); a title hit's snippet is the note's heading, which just repeats the title; matching is a plain substring test, so "day" matches "today" and every significant word of a message becomes its own query, attaching unrelated notes; results are first-come, not ranked (a note matching three of the message's words does not outrank one matching a single generic word), with up to seven full walks of the vault per turn; and a follow-up with no searchable words grounds nothing.

To measure instead of guess, a retrieval eval was built first: a small synthetic vault of plain notes in varied styles (heading-first, frontmatter, tag-only, daily notes, one long note, similar names) and 17 scored chat messages saying what must, and must not, reach the model, plus one documented limit. Retrieval is deterministic, so the eval needs no model and runs as ordinary tests. The synthetic vault is read through the real snapshot loader the nebula and search already use, so a pass exercises the real path. **Baseline against the current retriever: 7 of 17.** The failures are the ones above: generic words grounding unrelated notes, absent topics grounding noise, and title or tag hits arriving without their content.

## Decision

Grounding gets its own retriever. It is a plain, deterministic algorithm: no model call, no sub-agent (excluded by the project's non-goals), no embeddings, no persisted index.

**Passages, not notes.** Each note is split into passages (paragraphs within heading sections, long paragraphs cut at sentence boundaries, a few hundred characters each), and every passage carries its note's title, path, and nearest heading. What reaches the model is the passage text, so a hit on a note's title or tag brings real content, not a heading.

**Keyword scoring (BM25-style).** The message is tokenized on word boundaries (so "day" no longer matches "today"), filler words are dropped, and each term is weighted by how rare it is across the vault's notes: a word in two notes is a strong clue, one in a third of them is nearly none. A term's count in a passage also counts its appearances in the note's title, tags, and heading, at higher weight than body text, so a title hit boosts that note's passages. A term found in more than a quarter of the notes is ignored outright once the vault has at least ten notes (below that, frequencies are too small to mean anything). Plural "s" is folded so "flights" matches "flight". Filler includes the product's own vocabulary ("note", "vault"), which is in nearly every message to a notes assistant and says nothing about which note is meant.

**Precision over recall.** A wrong note is worse than no note: tested on the default model, irrelevant context derailed the reply (it answered the context instead of the question), while no context only produces a normal reply, and telling the model the context was keyword-matched and might be irrelevant, and to ignore what did not help, changed nothing. So the guard is in retrieval. A passage is grounded only on strong evidence: the message matches its note's title, tags, or heading; or two distinct message words appear in it; or the message has just one informative word (so "who is Priya?" still finds Priya). A lone ordinary word matching one body sentence ("create", "called") is not enough. Passages are then ranked by score, at most two per note, and only those scoring at least a set fraction of the best are kept, so a strong hit is not diluted by a tail of weak ones. If nothing qualifies, nothing is grounded, which the prompt renders as "no vault notes matched": an honest empty beats noise.

**One pass, cached.** The passage index is built from the already-cached vault snapshot and cached the same mtime-keyed way, replacing up to seven walks per turn with a lookup.

**Source-agnostic core.** Index building and scoring take a list of notes and know nothing about vaults or personas. Only the thin `ground()` wrapper resolves the persona's sandbox and loads the vault. This keeps the door open, without building it, for pointing the same retriever at a second, read-only source such as Sympose's own reference documentation (the basis for a persona with expert product knowledge), kept separate from the user's private vault.

**Prompt block.** The grounding block renders passages (title, path, heading, text) instead of one 70-character line. The grounding rules from ADR 012 and 006 stay engine-level and after the soul.

`vault_search.search_structured` is untouched and still serves the search bar.

## Consequences

**Measured.** Against the eval, 7 of 17 scored cases passed with the old retriever and 19 of 19 pass now (the eval grew by two cases and two documented gaps as testing found new failure classes). Real-model checks on `gemma2:9b` with the fixture vault: "tell me one thing about the Atlas project", "what did we decide about the database", and "should I switch Atlas to Postgres" now all carry the SQLite decision text to the model (before, only the heading "Atlas" or an unrelated journal line reached it); "hey, how are you today?" and "rough day, my brain is fried" ground nothing and get natural replies; a request to create a persona, which the first version of the retriever grounded on two unrelated notes and derailed, now grounds nothing and is declined honestly 3 of 3.

**Cost.** With the index cached, a turn's retrieval took about 8 ms on a synthetic 3,000-note vault (40,000 passages). Building the index takes about 0.9 s at that size (including reading and parsing the vault) and happens on the first turn and after any edit to the vault, since the whole index is rebuilt when the snapshot changes. The grounding block in the prompt is larger (up to five passages of a few hundred characters, against five 70-character lines), which costs prompt-processing time and is visible in the per-turn TTFT now recorded (ADR 013).

**Not indexed, by choice.** Passages with no real words (a `---` rule, a code fence marker) are dropped, because with a title boost and near-zero length they would outscore real text. Only a note's body is passage-indexed: a note with an empty body, and facts that live only in frontmatter properties, cannot be grounded (the old search matcher excluded frontmatter for the same reason: a value like `status: keyword` surfacing as prose the user never wrote). The failure mode is an honest "couldn't find it", not a fabrication; indexing properties is a possible later addition. The passage index cache has no lock, so two personas' first turns after a vault edit can both rebuild it (wasted CPU, never wrong), and one entry per persona scope is kept, the same small accepted-growth shape as other per-scope caches in the project.

**Search and grounding now diverge.** ADR 002's "improving snippet quality or match classification benefits both at once" no longer holds for grounding, which trades that for a retriever built for its actual input (a full chat sentence) and output (evidence for a model). Both still read the same cached snapshot and sandbox, so they always see the same notes.

**Known limits** ((1) and (2) are kept visible in the eval as documented gaps; (3) was observed live): (1) a message sharing no words with the note it should find ("what storage engine did we pick?" for a note saying "SQLite"), the ceiling of literal-word matching, where meaning-based matching (embeddings, as a hybrid) is the natural next step; the case is marked to fail loudly when it starts passing so the marker gets removed. (2) A common word that happens to be a title word still attaches a note when the distinctive word of the message is absent from the vault ("help me plan dinner" finds a note titled "Plan"). (3) A message that points at something unseen ("that layout") is grounded on a note literally titled "Layout" and the model presumes it is the one meant; asking instead did not work through instructions on the small model.

**Found along the way, not caused by this change:** when nothing is grounded, the small model sometimes says "I can't see your notes" (about 3 of 5 runs, and no better with the old retriever) instead of "I couldn't find that in your vault". A clearer empty-context sentence was tried and did not clearly help (and made a capability question worse in a small sample), so it was not kept; this is a model-behavior weakness to revisit, not solved here.

Amended by ADR 021: a hit that rests on a single matched word is now put to the rewrite step (ADR 017), and typed contractions and the persona's own name no longer count as evidence. Follow-up messages that carry no searchable words are not solved here; that is the next, separate slice (using recent turns as context), built as ADR 017 (retrieve first, and on a miss with history rewrite the message into a standalone query with one model call, behind a knob).

## Alternatives rejected

- **Keep patching the shared matcher** (longer snippets, skip the repeated heading, whole-word matching). Rejected: the ranking and multi-query problems remain, and every change has to serve the search bar's very different needs at the same time.
- **SQLite full-text search (FTS5) with BM25.** Available with no new dependency, but it wants a persisted index kept in sync with every vault edit, which ADR 002 already declined at personal-vault scale. Scoring in memory over the snapshot the process already caches gives the same idea with no index to maintain.
- **Embeddings or a reranking model.** A materially bigger piece of infrastructure (an embedding model, a store, re-embedding on every edit) before the simpler mechanism has been shown insufficient; the eval's documented gap is how we will know.
- **A model deciding what is relevant** (a rewriter, or a sub-agent). Costs a round trip on every turn, is slower and less predictable, and is excluded for sub-agents by the project's non-goals; the round-trip variant belongs to the follow-up slice, where it can be weighed as a reliability-versus-cost choice.
- **Putting whole notes in the prompt.** Simple, but unbounded in size for a small local model, and it buries the relevant paragraph.

**Update (ADR 024):** a passage now qualifies only when the message names at least half of one of its labels (title, a tag, or its heading), or matches at least two of its words and two fifths of the message's informative words, or the message has one informative word. One ordinary word of a long title, or two words of a long request, no longer attach a note.
